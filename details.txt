CH-BT: COAXIAL ISOMORPHIC CORE-SHELL NANOWIRE INTERCONNECT ARCHITECTURE
===============================================================================

1. FIELD OF THE INVENTION AND TARGET BOTTLENECK
This disclosure details a physical layout template for high-leverage Backend-of-Line 
(BEOL) logic nodes and interconnect structures at sub-2nm scales. Traditional 
monatomic metals (e.g., Copper, Cobalt, Ruthenium) fail catastrophically at 
sub-2nm bounds because the physical cross-sectional boundaries drop significantly 
below the material's bulk electron Mean Free Path (MFP), triggering severe 
boundary and impurity scattering. The resulting resistivity spike causes 
exponential, destructive localized Joule heating. 

2. MATERIAL EXPANSION: BROAD MATERIAL CLASSES
To solve this boundary limit, this architecture defines a multi-layered, 
coaxial heterostructure consisting of a high-MFP low-dimensional core enveloped 
by an isomorphic insulating shell layer. The structural claims extend across the 
following broad material classification definition:

  A. THE CONDUCTION CORE (Low-Dimensional Allotropic Conductors)
     * Primary Embodiment: An 8.4nm Multi-Walled Carbon Nanotube (MWCNT).
     * Extended Legal Coverage: Any low-dimensional, allotropic carbon matrix, 
       including single-walled carbon nanotubes (SWCNTs), multi-walled carbon 
       nanotubes (MWCNTs) ranging from 2nm to 15nm in outer diameter, linear 
       carbon allotropes (carbyne), and rolled multi-layer graphene nanoribbons (GNRs) 
       exhibiting 1D or quasi-1D quantum confinement.

  B. THE INSULATING PASSIVATION SHELL (Hexagonal Wide-Bandgap Dielectrics)
     * Primary Embodiment: A 4.8nm Hexagonal Boron Nitride (h-BN) jacket layer.
     * Extended Legal Coverage: Any structural group-III nitride matrix or 
       isomorphous 2D layered hexagonal crystal system with a wide electronic bandgap 
       (Eg > 3.0 eV), including hexagonal boron nitride (h-BN), boron-carbon-nitride 
       allotropes (B_x C_y N_z), and related high-k/low-k isostructural insulating 
       van der Waals heterostructures layered radially around a central conduction core.

3. PHYSICAL CHANNEL REGIME AND MECHANICS
The radial engineering configuration locks the electronic wavefunctions within 
the core channel, eliminating chemical dangling bonds and pinning the surface states. 
By selecting core-shell dimensions where the transport segment path (L) is engineered 
to be significantly shorter than the effective operational Mean Free Path of the 
allotropic core conductor (L << λ_eff), the structural node forces a Landauer-Büttiker 
quantum transport state. Conduction is governed strictly by the number of open 
transverse quantum modes (M), preventing acoustic and optical phonon-electron 
collisions, thereby suppressing localized Joule heating across all sub-2nm nodes.
